# ADR 0022: bounded broker subscriptions

Date: 8 September 2026. Status: implemented; all 193 full-repository checks, including 43 durable-delivery checks, and deployed crash/overflow/mandatory-return/causal-trace regressions passed; sustained performance qualification pending.

After the adapter route-lock correction, `load-diagnostic-1788896182801` recorded no PostgreSQL deadlocks but reached only 73.5% within two seconds of original eligibility (p99 16,552.934 ms). A second, explicitly instrumented diagnostic, `load-diagnostic-1788896633958`, reached 88.5% (p99 7,802.391 ms), again without deadlocks. These two-minute diagnostics are not A50 qualification. The latter added 143 database-wait samples and 29 nonfatal adapter thread dumps; that extra diagnostic overhead is excluded from the formal workload procedure.

Representative retained traces place most delay between publication of an original movement and its adapter allocation. The sampled consumer repeatedly waits inside synchronous `basicGet` calls. The database samples contain no query older than 200 ms or waiting on another backend, and adapter CPU-throttling counters did not increase during the sample. These observations motivate removing per-message broker round trips; they do not establish that polling explains every outlier. RabbitMQ recommends long-lived consumers over individual-message polling. [Consumer documentation](https://www.rabbitmq.com/docs/consumers)

Each `RabbitDelivery` now holds one subscription on its own channel. Broker prefetch and a bounded callback buffer both use the caller's limit: 16 in the runtime, with an API maximum of 32. Callbacks only transfer bytes into that buffer. The existing worker performs validation, durable inbox ownership and business handlers. A batch collects arrivals for at most 50 ms, then commits without waiting to fill the limit. This retains bounded transaction grouping while removing a broker request/reply for each delivery. [Prefetch documentation](https://www.rabbitmq.com/docs/consumer-prefetch)

Acknowledgements use the exact receiving channel and occur individually only after the inbox transaction succeeds. On admission failure, interruption, channel failure, process shutdown or an explicit consumer pause, the channel closes and every unacknowledged original can be redelivered. Already committed peers remain protected by original IDs and business uniqueness. A replacement subscription has its own buffer and delivery tags; old callbacks cannot acknowledge or populate the new session. A paused consumer releases prefetched work back to the broker, preserving queue-capacity and maintenance behavior. [Acknowledgement documentation](https://www.rabbitmq.com/docs/confirms)

At most 16 deliveries are unacknowledged per runtime subscription. The callback buffer and processing list each have a maximum of 16; acknowledged bodies may remain in the current list briefly while new callbacks arrive. With the existing 64-KiB body bound, those two containers retain at most 2 MiB of raw bodies, plus the bounded inbox transaction's copies and decoding overhead. Oversized or unexpectedly overflowing callbacks cause subscription teardown without acknowledgement. No thread waits for callback-buffer capacity.

The actual quorum tests verify eight prefetched originals and twelve broker-ready originals from a twenty-message burst, zero premature business effects, all twenty returned on pause, and twenty single effects after draining. A connection reset similarly returns the prefetched originals and recreates their acknowledgement channel. Existing commit-before-ack crashes, complete batch rollback, mandatory returns, duplicates, raw quarantine and storage rejection remain covered. The exact capacity error is asserted directly because this path no longer uses the old template callback's exception wrapper.

Contracts, schemas, identities, route epochs and database durability settings are unchanged. Full verification, deployed crash/overflow recovery and the unchanged ten-minute A50 target remain required before this decision is considered operationally qualified.
